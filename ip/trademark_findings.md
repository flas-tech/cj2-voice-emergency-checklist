## Detail on the decisive ones

- **VoxPilot — AVOID.** Identical wordmark, LIVE/REGISTERED in Class 9 for "software for reporting real-time analytics generated from interactive speech recognition applications" (Reg. No. 4551726, Ser. 86113375). Directly analogous to our voice-recognition product → near-certain refusal and opposition risk.
- **AeroVox — moderate.** LIVE Class 9 registration for capacitors (CD Aero LLC, Ser. 77409823). Different goods, but Class 9 is broad and an examiner could cite it.
- **Wingman — crowded.** 20+ live Class 9 marks (sunglasses, flotation vests, speakers, automotive driver-assist, etc.). Notably, the only *aviation-specific* Wingman marks — Beeper Avionics' "portable electronic device for detecting/locating aircraft" (Ser. 88947677) and "software for generating audible aircraft warnings/checklists" (Ser. 88984394) — are both DEAD/ABANDONED. The aviation niche is technically open but the name is weak and heavily contested.
- **AviVox / VoxCheck / CheckCall — clear.** Zero results in any class. Safest to register; strong inherent distinctiveness (coined/suggestive).
- **Litany — clear but weak.** No 9/42 conflicts, but it's a common English word (low distinctiveness, hard to enforce).

## Recommendation (for the report)
Lead candidate: **AviVox** (coined, aviation + voice, clean across all classes). Backups: **VoxCheck**, **CheckCall**. All three are inherently distinctive and currently unregistered. Confirm common-law/domain use before adoption.
