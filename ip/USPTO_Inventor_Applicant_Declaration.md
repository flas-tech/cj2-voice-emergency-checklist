This application names **two joint inventors**. Both must be named on the cover sheet and (at the nonprovisional stage) each must sign a declaration. Because the **inventors (individuals)** and the **applicant (an LLC)** are different parties, three things follow:

1. **A provisional does NOT require an inventor's oath/declaration.** Under 37 CFR 1.51(c), a provisional needs only the cover sheet + spec + fee + drawings. The formal inventor declaration (PTO/AIA/01) is required later, at the **nonprovisional** stage, and **each joint inventor signs their own**. So you do **not** need signed declarations to file the provisional. The data below is prepared so it's ready for the nonprovisional and so the chain of title is clean from day one.
2. **The LLC can only own the invention via assignments from BOTH inventors.** A second inventor means the LLC needs an assignment from each of Michael Gravalec and Brandon Cole. See Section C.
3. **Joint-inventor default ownership:** absent an assignment, each joint inventor owns an undivided interest in the whole and could independently license/practice it. This is exactly why getting both assignments to the LLC early matters.

## B. Entity Status — the critical decision (affects fee AND validity)

The provisional fee depends on entity status. Current USPTO fees (verify on uspto.gov before filing — they change annually):

| Status | Provisional fee (37 CFR 1.16(d)) | Extra form needed |
|---|---|---|
| Large entity | **$320** | none |
| **Small entity** | **$160** | none (just check the box) |
| **Micro entity** | **$65** | **Form PTO/SB/15A** (gross-income basis) |

### Why this is NOT simple — and a second inventor makes micro entity HARDER

Micro-entity status is **not** based only on income. With **two joint inventors** plus an **LLC applicant/assignee**, the qualifying tests apply to **each inventor individually AND to the LLC** — every one of them must pass:

- **Small-entity requirement:** the LLC must qualify as a small entity (≤500 employees, and not have assigned/licensed rights to a non-small entity). For a small startup LLC this is usually fine.
- **Four-application limit:** **neither** Michael Gravalec **nor** Brandon Cole **nor** the LLC may have been named on **more than 4** prior **non-provisional** U.S. applications (provisionals, PCT, and prior-employer-assigned apps don't count). **[CONFIRM for BOTH inventors — has either Michael or Brandon been named on >4 prior nonprovisionals?]**
- **Gross-income limit (~$251,190 for 2026 — verify current figure):** this must be satisfied **individually** by **ALL** of:
  - **Michael Gravalec** — his personal gross income in the preceding calendar year, **and**
  - **Brandon Cole** — his personal gross income in the preceding calendar year, **and**
  - the **applicant LLC** (Flite Line Aviation Services, LLC) — its own gross income, **and**
  - any entity the rights are assigned/obligated to.
- **If ANY one** of the two inventors or the LLC exceeds the limit, **micro entity is NOT available** — even if the others qualify. (The incomes are **not** combined; each is tested separately, but each must independently be under the cap.)

### Recommendation

- With two inventors there are now **more parties who can disqualify micro status**, so the risk of an improper micro claim is higher. If you are **not certain** that **both** inventors **and** the LLC are each below ~$251,190 for the preceding calendar year, **file as SMALL ENTITY ($160).** Improperly claiming micro entity can be treated as a **fee deficiency** and, in bad faith, raise inequitable-conduct concerns. The $95 difference is not worth the risk.
- File as **MICRO ENTITY ($65)** only if you can affirmatively certify (on Form SB/15A) that **all** of: small-entity status, the 4-application limit (both inventors), Michael's income, Brandon's income, AND the LLC's income are within limits.

**[DECIDE — Small ($160, no extra form, safe) vs Micro ($65, requires SB/15A + income certification for BOTH inventors and the LLC).]**

| When | Best practice: execute **before or at filing**; record at USPTO (via EPAS) — recording is optional for a provisional but recommended once a nonprovisional is filed |
| Recording fee | Electronic assignment recording is currently **$0** for patents via EPAS (verify) |

Until **both** inventors have assigned, the LLC does **not** hold the entire interest — a missing second assignment is a common chain-of-title gap. **A patent attorney should draft/review both assignments.**
